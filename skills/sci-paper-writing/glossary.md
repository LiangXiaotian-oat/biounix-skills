# Glossary

**Active voice** - Subject performs the action (Ch 2)
**Balance** - Symmetrical phrasing creating elegance (Ch 8)
**Characters** - The actors in a sentence; should be subjects (Ch 2)
**Clarity** - Writing that does not confuse; subjects=characters, verbs=actions (Ch 2)
**Coherence** - Paragraph-level consistency through topic and thematic strings (Ch 5)
**Cohesion** - Sentence-level flow through topic strings (Ch 3)
**Concision** - Expressing meaning in fewest words (Ch 6)
**Coordination** - Joining equal segments with and, but, yet, or (Ch 7)
**Discussion** - Manuscript section interpreting results (Ch 10)
**Due to** - Use only modifying nouns, never verbs (Ch 9)
**Emphasis** - Controlling what readers notice through sentence structure (Ch 4)
**Elegance** - Balance, symmetry, rhythm, metaphor beyond clarity (Ch 8)
**Folklore rules** - Rules that are not linguistic facts (Ch 9)
**Free modifiers** - Phrases after main clause without conjunctions (Ch 7)
**Inflated phrases** - Wordy substitutes: due to the fact that = because (Ch 6)
**Introduction** - Broad context to specific contribution (Ch 10)
**Issue** - Short opening segment of a paragraph (Ch 5, 12)
**Local clarity** - Clarity within individual sentences (Ch 3)
**Methods** - Reproducible experimental detail (Ch 10)
**Metaphor** - Figurative language making abstract concrete (Ch 8)
**Nominalization** - Converting verb to noun: decide -> decision (Ch 2)
**Old information** - Familiar concepts beginning sentences (Ch 3)
**Passive voice** - Subject receives the action (Ch 2)
**Point sentence** - Sentence stating the main claim of a paragraph (Ch 5)
**Redundant categories** - large in size where noun implies category (Ch 6)
**Redundant modifiers** - future plans where modifier adds nothing (Ch 6)
**Redundant pairs** - each and every (Ch 6)
**Reproducibility** - Research must be replicable (Ch 1)
**Resumptive modifiers** - Repeating key word to extend sentence (Ch 7)
**Results** - Findings without interpretation (Ch 10)
**Rhythm** - Pattern of long and short sentences (Ch 8)
**Sprawl** - Long sentences without structure (Ch 7)
**Stress position** - End of sentence; important new info (Ch 4)
**String of subjects** - Sequence of subjects across sentences (Ch 2)
**Study design** - Hypothesis, aims, methods, controls, statistics (Ch 10)
**Subordination** - Dependent clauses ranking ideas by importance (Ch 7)
**Summative modifiers** - Summarizing previous clause with noun, then extending (Ch 7)
**Symmetry** - Balanced phrasing creating elegance (Ch 8)
**Thematic string** - Recurring concept threading through sentences (Ch 5)
**Title** - Specific, informative, no abbreviations, full scientific names (Ch 10)
**Topic position** - Beginning of sentence; familiar info (Ch 4)
**Topic string** - Chain of sentence-opening topics (Ch 3)
**Trimming the end** - Deleting trailing words to expose key points (Ch 4)
**Turgid** - Writing full of abstract nouns and passive verbs (Ch 2)
**Usage** - Social convention in language (Ch 9)
**Williams theory** - Cognitive science of reading and writing (Ch 12)
