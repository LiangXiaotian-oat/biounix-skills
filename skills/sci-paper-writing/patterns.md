# Patterns and Techniques

## Who-Does-What Revision (Ch 2)
When: Any sentence feels unclear or abstract.
How: 1) Identify characters. 2) Identify actions. 3) Characters -> subjects. 4) Actions -> verbs. 5) Rearrange chronologically.

## Active-vs-Passive Decision (Ch 2)
When: Choosing voice.
How: 1) Must audience know who acts? 2) Is subject string consistent? 3) Is it the right string?

## Old-to-New Sentence Design (Ch 3)
When: Connecting sentences in a paragraph.
How: End with new info; begin next with that now-familiar info; end with newer info. Repeat.

## Trim-Shift-Emphasize (Ch 4)
When: A sentence ends weakly.
How: 1) Trim unnecessary trailing words. 2) Shift less important phrases left. 3) Use punctuation to mark emphasis.

## Theme Signaling (Ch 5)
When: Introducing a new topic.
How: Place new themes near the end (stress position) to signal what comes next.

## Concision Editing (Ch 6)
When: Any sentence feels wordy.
How: 1) Delete redundant pairs. 2) Delete redundant modifiers. 3) Delete redundant categories. 4) Replace inflated phrases. 5) Delete meaningless intros. 6) Delete what readers can infer.

## Sentence Extension (Ch 7)
When: Including complex info without losing clarity.
How: 1) Coordination after subject. 2) Subordination for ranked ideas. 3) Resumptive modifiers. 4) Summative modifiers.

## Manuscript Writing Order (Ch 10)
When: Starting a new manuscript.
How: Methods -> Results -> Introduction -> Discussion -> Conclusion -> Title -> Abstract.

## Abstract Structuring (Ch 10)
When: Writing or revising an abstract.
How: Background (context+purpose), Results (main findings), Conclusions (implications). Max 350 words. No references.

## Title Optimization (Ch 10)
When: Writing a title.
How: Specific terms. Simple word order. No abbreviations. Full scientific names. No Roman numerals.

## Table Design (Ch 11)
When: Presenting numerical data.
How: If data fits one sentence, do not use a table. Give only significant figures. Omit unessential data. Watch for excessive zeros.

## Revision Response (Ch 11)
When: Responding to reviewer comments.
How: Point-by-point response. Explain disagreement with data or citations. Mark changes in manuscript.

## Conference Slides (Ch 11)
When: Preparing presentation slides.
How: Sans serif font. Black on white. One idea per slide. 4-second comprehension rule. Never read slides to audience.

## Introduction Funnel (Ch 10, 14)
When: Writing an introduction.
How: Context (broad) -> Transition (gap) -> Point (your contribution) -> Significance. See rice blast Cell paper example in ch14.
