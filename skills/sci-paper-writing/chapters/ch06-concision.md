# Chapter 6: Concision

## Core Idea
Clear structure is not enough - sentences must be concise. Two principles: (1) compress meaning into fewest words, (2) do not state what readers can infer.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two Principles of Concision**: 1) Compress what you mean into the fewest words. 2) Do not state what your reader can easily infer.
- **Common Wordiness Patterns**: Redundant pairs, redundant modifiers, redundant categories, meaningless phrases, inflated phrases.
- **Compression Techniques**: Delete redundant words, replace phrases with single words, convert clauses to phrases.

## Key Concepts
- **Redundant pairs**: each and every, first and foremost - pick one.
- **Redundant modifiers**: completely finish, future plans - modifier adds nothing.
- **Redundant categories**: large in size, round in shape - noun implies category.
- **Meaningless phrases**: in my personal opinion, it is important to note that - cut them.
- **Inflated phrases**: due to the fact that (because), in the event that (if).

## Mental Models
- One word test: can a phrase be replaced by a single word? If yes, do it.
- Signal-to-noise ratio: delete noise, keep signal.
- Reader inference test: if the reader already knows it, do not say it.

## Anti-patterns
- Padding: It should be noted that... It is interesting to observe that...
- Inflated phrases: due to the fact that instead of because
- Redundant modifiers: absolutely essential, completely eliminate

## Worked Example
Before: The point I want to make here is that we can see that American policy in regard to foreign countries as the State Department in Washington and the White House have put it together and made it public to the world has given material and moral support to too many foreign factions in other countries that have controlled power and have then had to give up the power to other factions that have defeated them.
After: American foreign policy has backed too many losers.

Analysis: 60+ words compressed to 6. Deleted every word the reader can infer.

## Key Takeaways
1. Compress meaning into the fewest words
2. Never state what readers can easily infer
3. Watch for redundant pairs, modifiers, and categories
4. Replace inflated phrases with single words
5. Delete meaningless introductory phrases
