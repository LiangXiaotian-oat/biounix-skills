# Chapter 3: Cohesion

## Core Idea
Local clarity is not enough. Sentences must fit their context, reflect a consistent point of view, and emphasize important ideas. Cohesion comes from managing topic strings.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Topic Strings**: Each sentence begins with a topic connecting to the previous sentence. Readers expect old information at the beginning and new information at the end.
- **Old-to-New Principle**: Begin with familiar/known information, end with new information. This creates a bridge from what readers know to what they do not.
- **Consistent Point of View**: Maintain a stable sequence of subjects across sentences.

## Key Concepts
- **Topic**: The first few words of a sentence - what it is about. Usually the grammatical subject.
- **Stress**: The end of a sentence - where new and important information goes.
- **Topic string**: The chain of topics across consecutive sentences creating flow.
- **Local clarity**: Clear sentences individually, which may still be confusing in sequence.

## Mental Models
- Use old before new: start each sentence with familiar info, end with new info.
- Think of paragraphs as chains: each sentence hooks into the previous one.
- Use the topic string test: list first few words of each sentence to see flow.

## Anti-patterns
- **Inconsistent subjects**: Jumping between topics without transition.
- **New information at the beginning**: Starting with unfamiliar concepts.
- **Ignoring context**: Clear sentences that do not connect.

## Worked Example
Before: Asian competitors who have sought to compete directly with Acme's X-line product groups in each of six market segments will constitute the main objective of the first phase of this study.
After: The first phase of this study will mainly examine six market segments in the Western Pacific region to determine how Asian competitors have sought to compete directly with Acme's X-line product groups.

Analysis: Version b opens with familiar topic and ends with new information. Version a buries the main point.

## Key Takeaways
1. Begin sentences with old/familiar information, end with new
2. Maintain consistent strings of subjects across sentences
3. Local clarity does not guarantee readability
4. The first few words set reader expectations
5. Topic strings create the flow for coherent paragraphs
