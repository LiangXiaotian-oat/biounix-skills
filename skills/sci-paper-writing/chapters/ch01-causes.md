# Chapter 1: Causes - Why Scientific Writing Matters

## Core Idea
Scientific writing is not optional - it is the primary metric by which scientists are evaluated. Bad writing delays or prevents publication of good science.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Publication as the Goal of Science**: An experiment is not complete until results are published. Reproducibility is the key word.
- **The Imitation Problem**: Many Chinese scientists learned writing by imitating predecessors, perpetuating errors "in perpetuity."

## Key Concepts
- **Reproducibility**: The defining characteristic of science - research must be written so others can replicate it.
- **Communication Arts**: Often neglected in science education, leading to good scientists being poor writers.
- **The scientist must not only "do" science but must "write" science.**

## Mental Models
- Use the "publication = completion" model: no paper, no science.
- Think of writing as part of the experiment, not an afterthought.

## Anti-patterns
- **Imitating without understanding**: Copying style from previous authors propagates their defects.
- **Neglecting writing skills**: Focusing only on technical aspects while ignoring communication.

## Worked Example
The book opens with a GM crops paragraph full of errors: passive voice, abstract nouns, missing agents. The Pre-curriculum test asks students to correct underlined parts. This sets the baseline: if you can spot the errors, you are qualified to continue.

## Key Takeaways
1. Scientists are measured by publications, not lab skills alone
2. Bad writing can prevent good science from being published
3. Writing must be learned systematically, not by imitation
4. Reproducibility requires clear written documentation
