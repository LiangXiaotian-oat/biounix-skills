# Chapter 9: Usage

## Core Idea
Good grammar and appropriate usage are different from clarity. Some rules are real linguistic facts; others are classroom folklore. Know which rules matter.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Grammar vs Usage vs Choice**: Grammar = linguistic fact. Usage = social convention. Choice = stylistic preference.
- **Real Rules vs Folklore**: Many rules taught in classrooms are not linguistic facts but historical preferences.
- **Three Levels of Correctness**: (1) Rules everyone agrees on (irregardless is wrong). (2) Rules some enforce but good writers ignore (split infinitives, preposition at end). (3) Genuine stylistic choices.

## Key Concepts
- **Folklore rules**: Do not begin with and/but. Do not end with preposition. Do not split infinitives.
- **Real rules**: Subject-verb agreement, correct tense, proper pronoun case.
- **Due to vs Because of**: Use due to only in phrases modifying nouns, never modifying verbs.

## Mental Models
- Three-circle test: real rule (inner), debated rule (middle), or choice (outer)?
- Usage as social convention: follow audience expectations but do not be bound by folklore.
- Clarity override: when folklore conflicts with clarity, choose clarity.

## Anti-patterns
- Blind rule-following: applying folklore rules even when they make writing worse.
- Ignoring real rules: subject-verb agreement, incorrect tense.
- Confusing choice with error: treating stylistic preference as grammatical error.

## Worked Example
Folklore: Do not split infinitives. To accurately measure the response vs To measure the response accurately. Both correct; splitting is clearer when modifier belongs to verb.

Real rule: data is plural in scientific writing - use data show, not data shows.

Usage: irregardless is never acceptable; use regardless.

Due to: This might be partly due to the strict regulatory constraints (correct - modifies noun). It failed due to poor planning (incorrect - should be because of).

## Key Takeaways
1. Distinguish real grammar rules from classroom folklore
2. Split infinitives when doing so improves clarity
3. Ending sentences with prepositions is natural and acceptable
4. Beginning sentences with and or but is legitimate for emphasis
5. In scientific writing, data is plural - use data show
6. Use due to only to modify nouns, not verbs
