# Chapter 2: Clarity

## Core Idea
Clear writing follows two principles: (1) subjects name characters, (2) verbs name actions. Convert abstract nouns back to verbs and make actors into subjects.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two Principles of Clear Writing**: 1) Subjects name the cast of characters. 2) Verbs name the crucial actions.
- **Characters and Actions**: Every story has characters and actions. Match characters to subjects, actions to verbs.
- **Nominalization Problem**: Abstract nouns (e.g. determination, effectiveness) hide actions. Convert them back to verbs.
- **Active vs Passive Choice**: Three questions: Must audience know who acts? Is subject string consistent? Is it the right string?

## Key Concepts
- **Nominalization**: Turning a verb into a noun (decide -> decision), obscuring action and actor.
- **Turgid style**: Writing full of abstract nouns, passive verbs, displaced actors.
- **Useful Nominalizations**: Some are legitimate when referring to the concept itself.

## Mental Models
- Use Who does what? as editing lens: identify characters, make them subjects; identify actions, make them verbs.
- Think of a sentence as a story: it needs characters (subjects) performing actions (verbs).
- Use the string of subjects test: trace subjects across sentences to check consistency.

## Anti-patterns
- **Excessive nominalization**: Our lack of knowledge precluded determination of committee action effectiveness.
- **Disjointed sentences**: Parts that should be together are separated.
- **Unnecessary passives**: Obscuring agency when audience needs to know.

## Worked Example
Before: Our lack of knowledge about local conditions precluded determination of committee action effectiveness in fund allocation to those areas in greatest need of assistance.
After: Because we knew nothing about local conditions, we could not determine how effectively the committee had allocated funds to areas that most needed assistance.

Analysis: lack of knowledge -> we knew nothing; precluded determination -> could not determine; committee action effectiveness -> how effectively the committee had allocated.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make subjects name characters and verbs name actions
2. Convert abstract nominalizations back to active verbs
3. Choose active voice when audience needs to know who acts
4. Check for consistent strings of subjects across sentences
5. Some nominalizations are useful - keep those referring to concepts
