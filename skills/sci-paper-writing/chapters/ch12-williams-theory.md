# Chapter 12: Williams - Style: Toward Clarity and Grace

## Core Idea
Joseph M. Williams classic writing theory. The course PPT (ch01-ch10) is derived from this book. Williams provides the theoretical depth behind the principles: why clarity works, how readers process sentences, and the cognitive science of reading.

## Key Theoretical Contributions

### The Problem with Standard Advice
- Standard books say Be clear and Omit Needless Words but never explain HOW.
- Williams goes beyond platitudes to a coherent system of principles.

### Why Clarity Works: The Reader's Perspective
- Readers process sentences by identifying characters and actions.
- When subjects name characters and verbs name actions, readers process efficiently.
- Nominalizations force readers to mentally reconstruct actors and actions, increasing cognitive load.

### Old and New Information
- Readers expect to see familiar information at the beginning of sentences.
- New information at the end creates a bridge from known to unknown.
- This is not stylistic preference - it is how cognitive processing works.

### Issue and Discussion (Williams Coherence II)
- Paragraphs have an issue (short opening) and discussion (longer development).
- The end of the issue signals what the discussion will cover.
- Principle 4: A reader feels a paragraph is coherent if they can identify a single clear point.

### Concision Theory
- Wordiness is not just verbose - it is a symptom of unclear thinking.
- Murder your darlings (Quiller-Couch): delete even passages you are proud of if they do not serve the reader.

### Metaphor and Elegance
- Metaphor can explain but also mislead: Societies give birth to new values through osmotic flow - mixed metaphor confuses.
- Fresh metaphors illuminate; stale metaphors (timid as a mouse) signal lazy thinking.

## Key Quotes from Williams
- Telling me to Be clear is like telling me to Hit the ball squarely. I know that. What I do not know is how to do it.
- We write a first draft for ourselves; the drafts thereafter increasingly for the reader.
- Prior knowledge matters: what counts most in comprehending a text is how much we already know about its content.

## Key Takeaways
1. Standard advice (be clear, be brief) is useless without explaining HOW
2. Readers cognitively process sentences through characters and actions
3. Old-new information flow is based on cognitive science, not preference
4. Paragraphs need a clear issue + discussion structure with a single point
5. Wordiness reflects unclear thinking, not just bad style
6. Metaphors can illuminate or confuse - use sparingly in science
