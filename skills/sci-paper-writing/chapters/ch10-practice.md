# Chapter 10: Practice - Manuscript Writing & Publication

## Core Idea
Scientific writing serves publication. Understand what journal editors want, write sections in optimal order, and navigate peer review.

## Optimal Writing Order
1. Methods (you know it best)
2. Results (record as data comes in)
3. Introduction (once target journal selected)
4. Discussion (interpret results)
5. Conclusion (summarize implications)
6. Title (distill essence)
7. Abstract (compress whole paper last)

## Title Guidelines
- Use specific terms (drug name, not drug class)
- Use simple word order and common word combinations
- Avoid abbreviations and acronyms
- Write scientific names in full: Escherichia coli, not E. coli
- Refer to chemicals by common/generic name
- Avoid Roman numerals

## Abstract Guidelines
- Max 350 words; structured: Background, Results, Conclusions
- Include synonyms for title keywords
- Minimize abbreviations; no references
- Check consistency with final manuscript

## Introduction Structure
- Known -> Unknown -> Your contribution
- Move from broad context to specific purpose
- Cite relevant literature; state the gap

## Methods
- Provide reproducible detail
- Include: study design, materials, procedures, statistics
- Use past tense

## Results
- Present findings without interpretation
- Use tables and figures strategically
- Do not repeat table/figure content in text

## Discussion
- Interpret results, compare with literature
- Acknowledge limitations
- Structure: specific results -> broader implications -> future directions

## Publishing Process (from Day)
- Determine authors before writing
- Start writing before experiments complete
- Select target journal by scope, audience, impact factor
- Editor decision: accept (~5%), reject, or modify
- Revision: address reviewer comments point by point
- Proof review: check galleys carefully

## Worked Example: Introduction from Cell paper on rice blast
- Context: Rice feeds half the world population; rice blast causes significant crop loss.
- Transition: Non-race-specific resistance is more effective due to broad spectrum and durability.
- Point: We identified a natural allele of a C2H2-type transcription factor conferring non-race-specific resistance.
- Significance: This allele offers a strategy for breeding durable resistance.

## Key Takeaways
1. Write Methods/Results first; Title/Abstract last
2. Titles: specific, full scientific names, no abbreviations
3. Abstracts: structured, max 350 words, no references
4. Introduction = funnel; Discussion = reverse funnel
5. Only ~5% accepted as submitted - expect revisions
6. Address reviewer comments with evidence, not emotion
